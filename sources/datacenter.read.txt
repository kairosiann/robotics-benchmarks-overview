UPS stands for uninterruptible power supply, the battery backup that keeps servers running through a power cut.
Explanation of each step in data-center manuals
Why slow models overheat
While a model thinks, the robot holds its arms in place, and holding still heats the motors. When a motor reaches 65 °C, our thermal guard trips and returns the arms to rest pose for cooling.
The longer a model thinks per move, the more tries overheat
How we chose the tasks
We drafted 19 candidate tasks, each matched to a step quoted from a manufacturer manual or standard (Vertiv,
          Eaton, Dell, OSHA, WAGO and StarTech). They fell into four groups: operating controls, connecting equipment,
          servicing equipment, and the skills electricians are examined on.
We kept ten single physical steps that a tabletop rig can reset and score from photos. That is six control tasks, one of them turning a key in an interlock, and four connection tasks.

Limitations
The benchmark tests individual physical steps, not complete maintenance jobs. It does not cover electrical
          isolation, termination, testing or fault-finding.
The props simplify the equipment, forces and access constraints. We have not validated performance on these setups against the real equipment.
Each task ran on one rig. Differences between rigs (camera angles, how firmly a prop was fixed down) are part
          of each task's difficulty and are not averaged out.
Three tries per model per task is a small sample. Scores a few points apart aren't a reliable ranking.
Some failures, like misreading a knob's angle, may come from the 224 × 224 camera resolution.
