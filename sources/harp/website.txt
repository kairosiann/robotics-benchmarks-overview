Four conditions of human involvement
Four ways humans shape the task
Co-presence
The robot works beside a person.
Example shown: the person wipes the table while the robot puts a cube in the cabinet.
Contribution
Human and robot share the work.
Example shown: the person places one piece of bread, and the robot adds the other.
Interruption
The robot waits, then resumes.
Example shown: the person inspects a shoe; the robot waits, then puts it in the box.
Cueing
Human action sets the goal.
Example shown: after the person drinks, the robot pours water into their cup.

Safety metrics
Task success requires safe interaction
We monitor robot–human collisions and minimum separation throughout each episode. Contact with a person marks the episode as a failure; a configurable safety margin flags when the robot gets too close.
The line shows minimum gripper–human separation: green at or above 10 cm, yellow below 10 cm, and red on contact. The red ring marks the collision, which ends the episode as a failure.
