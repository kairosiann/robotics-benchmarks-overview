Abstract
Although the advancement of vision-language models (VLMs) has endowed robots with enhanced environmental understanding and task reasoning, a comprehensive evaluation methodology is important to advance the integration of VLMs in robotic navigation and manipulation. However, current benchmarks lack a comprehensive method to evaluate diverse robotic tasks, and evaluation metrics remain relatively constrained, making it difficult to assess the embodied capabilities of VLMs in a thorough and fine-grained manner. To address this issue, we propose RMMBench, an evaluation benchmark that requires robots to understand language instructions and perform long-horizon tasks in continuous spaces. RMMBench seamlessly integrates high- and low-level embodied tasks into a unified framework, constructing a “navigation-manipulation” task suite comprising 90 canonical task scenarios that range from localized manipulation to long-horizon composite navigation. The results reveal that leading VLMs still face major challenges in spatial localization when performing mobile manipulation tasks, and also highlight the necessity of enhancing the spatial perception capability of robots during long-horizon interactions. RMMBench can be accessed at
https://mxxq-stack.github.io/rmmbench-project/

Introduction
The integration of natural language instructions to guide robotic navigation and manipulation tasks represents an ongoing pursuit in the robotics community
[
31
]
. Traditional learning-based policies and task and motion planning frameworks initially establish a classical pipeline that decouples high-level reasoning from low-level execution
[
20
,
37
,
15
,
33
,
11
]
. Recently, large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) have been widely adopted as high-level planners for robot learning
[
3
,
22
,
45
,
43
]
. This shift has led to the development of various benchmarks designed to evaluate these models in robotic tasks.
Early benchmarks focused primarily on discrete symbolic tracking and high-level abstract task planning
[
23
]
, limiting the output of a model to macro-commands or semantic goals. To test the proficiency of a model in handling diverse tasks, recent work introduced multi-stage, long-horizon benchmarks featuring various scenarios
[
29
,
42
]
. However, to isolate semantic reasoning or simplify evaluation pipelines, such benchmarks commonly employed encapsulated interaction middleware or separate vision components (such as SAM
[
32
]
or Grounding DINO
[
28
]
) to provide spatial grounding
[
14
,
26
,
41
]
.
Due to the inherent difficulty of alignment in navigation-to-manipulation transitions, existing benchmarks for more complex mobile manipulation tasks typically relied on underlying navigation modules for path finding and resorted to heuristic-based grasping
[
26
,
41
]
. However, this simplification fails to rigorously test the autonomous navigation capability of the models and their precision in execution. Consequently, they often relied on coarse-grained, outcome-oriented metrics (e.g., task success rates) for performance evaluation, leaving the continuous tracking of intermediate action execution unexamined. Most benchmarks characterized tasks through macro-level concepts such as “semantic understanding” or “commonsense reasoning”
[
42
,
14
,
41
]
. Without fine-grained execution metrics, the actual quality of the generated low-level actions remains unquantified, failing to provide detailed diagnostic insights. Therefore, a comprehensive benchmark based on raw visual inputs and continuous execution is required to analyze the capability of a model in low-level spatial reasoning and mobile manipulation.
To address such evaluation requirements and establish a more comprehensive diagnostic paradigm, this work introduces RMMBench, an evaluation benchmark for grounded spatial perception and composite mobile manipulation. Table
1
compares RMMBench with some popular benchmarks for embodied intelligence. We construct a “navigation-manipulation” task suite comprising 90 canonical task scenarios that range from localized manipulation to long-horizon composite navigation. Within this suite, a robot must perform discrete, grid-based navigation toward target waypoints and autonomously regulate its orientation for object alignment, while explicitly predicting pixel-wise bounding boxes (BBoxes) to facilitate subsequent manipulation. To enable effective decision-making in long-horizon tasks, RMMBench provides the complete textual history of past actions and environmental feedback, while restricting the visual context to a sliding window of recent observations to prevent context explosion. Through this operational interface, we establish a fine-grained evaluation protocol using 6 categories of process-oriented metrics to systematically analyze performance across these composite tasks.
The composite tasks in our benchmark are designed to evaluate embodied models in sequential navigation-manipulation coordination and long-horizon multi-goal planning.
Unlike existing benchmarks that rely on external navigation modules or predefined interaction triggers, we require models to autonomously perform navigation, spatial alignment, and manipulation based solely on continuous visual observations and environmental feedback.
This setting imposes strict requirements on low-level spatial grounding, temporal action consistency, and dynamic task scheduling in complex environments.
By explicitly modeling continuous execution processes and navigation-manipulation transitions, RMMBench enables fine-grained diagnosis of grounded spatial reasoning and low-level embodied decision-making beyond conventional task success metrics.
The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
•
We propose RMMBench, a comprehensive benchmark that directly assesses VLM-based methods for robotic navigation and manipulation from high-level language understanding and pixel-wise visual grounding to low-level action execution.
•
We establish a fine-grained, multidimensional evaluation protocol to systematically decouple high-level planning failures from low-level execution bottlenecks, enabling a more precise diagnostic analysis.
•
The evaluation results on RMMBench, which provides a “navigation-manipulation” task hierarchy comprising 90 canonical task scenarios, reveal that the spatial localization under complex visual conditions remains challenging for today’s leading VLMs.

Related Work
Embodied Manipulation Benchmarks.
The development of benchmarks and methodologies for embodied manipulation has transitioned from discrete symbolic planning to semantic reasoning driven by VLMs. Early long-horizon platforms
[
6
,
25
,
19
]
focused primarily on the construction of low-level skill chains, yet their task spaces were strictly limited to stationary tabletop or cabinet-level operations. Although some room-scale environments
[
25
,
35
]
extended the diversity of scenarios, their underlying physical interactions often relied on discrete symbolic interfaces.
Modular Planning Paradigms.
Embodied foundation models have diverged into end-to-end policies
[
2
,
24
,
1
]
and modular planning frameworks
[
31
,
21
]
. Nevertheless, state-of-the-art modular workflows
[
10
]
suffer from two key disconnections: one in perception and the other in interaction. On the perception side, they suffer from “perceptual fragmentation” by relying heavily on external, separate vision modules (e.g., SAM
[
32
]
, Grounding DINO
[
28
]
, or Set-of-Mark prompting
[
23
,
40
,
34
]
) to heuristically extract BBoxes. Feeding these as symbolic labels to the foundation model segregates semantic reasoning from native geometric spatial awareness. On the interaction side, existing platforms mostly lack deep closed-loop testing for the “perception-execution disconnect”. When a VLM generates a faulty plan or encounters an unreachable state, prominent zero-shot evaluation frameworks
[
21
,
10
]
typically terminate the current episode or rely on open-loop implicit corrections. To address these limitations, our work constructs a spatially extended “navigation-manipulation” composite task hierarchy, requiring models to coordinate navigation between task locations while maintaining native visual grounding during dynamic physical trial-and-error.
Procedural Evaluation Metrics.
Although recent evaluation environments
[
42
,
26
]
attempted to introduce diversified evaluation criteria, current frameworks still tend to favor macro-level, outcome-oriented metrics (such as final task success rates or coarse sub-task completion scores
[
23
,
13
,
17
,
16
,
9
]
) when quantifying subtle aspects such as redundant actions, wandering trajectories, or decision deadlocks. Such an evaluation bias easily obscures the procedural redundancy of agents during long-term execution. To bridge this gap, we propose a fine-grained diagnostic framework that achieves precise quantification of dynamic trial-and-error redundancy and subtle cognitive flaws in large models.

RMMBench
RMMBench requires VLM to execute continuous-space, long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks based on language instructions and raw visual feedback. Evaluating this capability requires a tight integration of sequential planning, spatial grounding, and closed-loop visual verification. In this section, we present the benchmark design: Sec.
3.1
defines our multi-paradigm task hierarchy and atomic skill libraries; Sec.
3.2
elaborates the comprehensive evaluation pipeline across navigation and manipulation boundaries; and Sec.
3.3
establishes the comprehensive suite for fine-grained performance evaluation.
3.1
Task Hierarchy and Atomic Skill Libraries
To evaluate the embodied perception, semantic reasoning, and zero-shot execution capabilities of models, we construct a randomized data synthesis pipeline within a unified MuJoCo
[
38
]
physics simulation environment supported by the dm_control
[
39
]
framework, leveraging RoboCasa
[
29
]
and Objaverse
[
7
]
assets. To improve physical plausibility, we apply geometric filtering to the raw assets and exclude objects whose scale makes them unsuitable for grasping. For composite navigation-manipulation tasks, the framework introduces diverse scenes from RoboCasa
[
29
]
across multiple functional layouts and aesthetic styles. To evaluate policy robustness under distribution shifts, each scenario dynamically randomizes target object categories, initial coordinates, and scene material textures during initialization.
RMMBench comprises 90 canonical task scenarios structured across two primary evaluation paradigms to comprehensively stress-test VLMs. The first paradigm encompasses 60 standalone manipulation scenarios (39 short-horizon and 21 long-horizon tasks), spanning from localized structured executions with deterministic routines to open-ended interactions driven by high-level guidance. Since contemporary VLMs already possess rich world knowledge, we embed commonsense and semantic considerations into the natural-language instructions of both short- and long-horizon tasks rather than curating them as a separate category, focusing evaluation on the more challenging long-horizon tasks. Complementing these localized tasks, the second paradigm introduces 30 composite navigation-manipulation scenarios that demand cross-space skill chaining across multiple functional layouts. Manipulation task examples are illustrated in
Figure
F.1
, and composite navigation-manipulation task examples in
Figure
B.1
, both in the supplementary material.
The core atomic skill library exposes discrete primitives including pick, place, pull, lift, push, and open_door/close_door, open_sink, and rotate_knob, alongside active ego-perception via wrist_observe. When a VLM selects a skill, the DSL
[
30
,
36
]
interface routes the command to a unified “RRT–IK–low-level controller tracking” execution pipeline: the VLM is responsible only for high-level decisions, including skill selection (with parameters), BBox prediction, and grasp candidate selection, while the backend handles RRT planning, IK solving, and low-level control. Skills such as lift and pull use preset default movement distances, and the VLM can increase the end-effector movement distance via re-execution.
3.2
Evaluation Pipeline
As illustrated in Figure

The framework provides the VLM with an interleaved context of text histories and visual streams, translating its macro-instructions into executable atomic skills, and conditionally routing explicit environmental feedback only when execution constraints are violated (e.g., collisions or out-of-reach errors).

Tabletop Manipulation.
During manipulation tasks, the VLM uses head and wrist cameras to predict 2D BBoxes for targets and containers, and localization accuracy is evaluated using the intersection-over-union (IoU) between the predicted BBoxes and the corresponding ground-truth masks. For grasp execution, the options projected onto the wrist view comprise a hybrid candidate pool containing both dynamically generated GraspNet predictions and expert-verified ground-truth grasp annotations associated with each object asset. The latter serves as an environmental baseline fallback to guarantee candidate availability across complex scenes. Specifically, the target pose for pick is provided by the 6-DoF grasp pose generated by GraspNet, whereas the placement coordinates for place are determined by the VLM-predicted BBox; grasp points are bound to object assets and mapped to world coordinates via transformation matrices following the object’s actual pose. Crucially, all pose candidates are anonymized and uniformly presented to the VLM. In tasks with confined spaces or specific directional constraints (e.g., horizontal or vertical approach requirements), GraspNet introduces necessary geometric directional diversity. The VLM must then autonomously perform zero-shot spatial reasoning to select the optimal option for low-level point-to-point path solvers.
Action History and Error Feedback.
To support evaluations of long-horizon tasks, RMMBench maintains the full textual history of past actions while restricting the visual context to a sliding window of recent frames. The simulator enforces strict physical reachability and kinematic constraints without applying any implicit state corrections (e.g., automatic object teleportation or base repositioning). In most cases, the environment relies entirely on the subsequent sensory update, and the VLM must autonomously verify the execution outcome, such as detecting an empty gripper or an undisplaced target, solely through its visual observation stream. The only exception is text-based feedback during pick and place execution, which is provided under two conditions: (1) the target lies outside the reachable workspace; or (2) the predicted BBox contains no valid point cloud. All other failures (e.g., collisions, slippage, anomalous object displacement) produce no textual hints and expose no internal simulator state to the model. This design compensates for the model’s lack of direct access to the robot’s kinematic constraints (e.g., arm length and reachable range), preventing the action budget from being exhausted on physically unreachable, ineffective operations. This non-oracle feedback mechanism rigorously tests whether the model can detect execution anomalies from raw vision and dynamically switch back to navigation or inspection primitives for repositioning and replanning.

Experiments
Our experimental design focuses on analyzing VLM behaviors across three core operational dimensions: (1) task progression on manipulation tasks of increasing complexity; (2) decentralized navigation capability relying solely on first-person active vision without global spatial priors; and (3) the stability and degradation patterns of 2D spatial grounding under long-horizon execution.
4.1
Experimental Setups and Overall Performance
We evaluate eight representative VLMs: Gemini-2.5-Pro
[
5
]
, Gemini-3-Pro-preview, Gemini-3-Flash-preview, Qwen-3.5-Plus, Doubao-SEED-2.0-Pro-260215, GPT-6-Astra, Kimi-K3, and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash. Tests are conducted in a unified MuJoCo simulation framework comprising 90 canonical task scenarios: 60 pure manipulation tasks and 30 composite navigation-manipulation tasks. Domain randomization is applied across these scenarios to support rigorous quantitative evaluation. Each task instance is evaluated over 3 independent episodes to guarantee statistical reliability. Task configurations are pre-generated as fixed JSON files, and all models load identical configurations to ensure fairness and reproducibility.
Experimental results demonstrate a clear decline in models’ performance as task horizon and complexity increase. The performance drops significantly when transitioning further to composite navigation-manipulation tasks that require extended skill sequences. Active Vision and Egocentric Navigation.
We conduct an ablation study to compare navigation performance with and without BEV mapping. The results listed in Tables
3
and
4
reveal a critical bottleneck: stripping away global top-down priors leads to a severe degradation in both navigation success rates and pathfinding efficiency across all models. Under purely egocentric constraints with discrete movement and inspection action spaces, the model tends to rely heavily on rotation primitives to keep the target object within its local field of view rather than proactively exploring the environment via forward advancement, which drastically increases action redundancy.
Furthermore, due to the lack of global spatial references, a VLM tends to prematurely issue localized manipulation commands before the mobile base reaches the workstation interaction radius, as shown in Figure
3
(d), some models persist in grasping targets that are visible yet beyond the operational range, repeatedly issuing identical pick commands instead of navigating closer, which is an indication of inadequate perception of their own operational boundaries. Similarly, as shown in Figure
3
(e), models fail to leverage historical visual context to avoid previously observed obstacles, still outputting pick followed by move_forward directly toward the obstacle. These failures expose the fragility of egocentric spatial reasoning and temporal visual memory in VLMs.
Spatial Grounding Degradation in Long Sequences.
We probe the effect of context length on localization accuracy via a controlled experiment that fixes the visual input and varies only the number of history frames in the context (2–16), with full results in Table
E.1
of the supplementary material. Localization accuracy varies in a clearly model-dependent manner: models such as Gemini-3-Flash remain largely stable, whereas only SEED-2.0 exhibits a monotonic degradation that emerges earlier and is exacerbated under the long-distance, occluded condition, indicating that context length alone is not the direct cause but interacts with scene complexity and model-dependent visual robustness.
5
Conclusion
We introduce RMMBench, a simulation benchmark for evaluating VLMs on grounded spatial perception and composite mobile manipulation, whose comprehensive evaluation suite decouples planning efficacy from localized execution failures to move beyond conventional outcome-oriented evaluation. Experimental results show that state-of-the-art VLMs, while possessing adequate affordance knowledge for short-horizon manipulation, exhibit weak egocentric pathfinding when detached from global top-down priors, with spatial localization robustness under long sequences being model-dependent—models sensitive to complex visual conditions degrade markedly as sequences accumulate. These findings reveal critical bottlenecks in modular VLM workflows and underscore the need for stronger temporal visual memory and dynamic error correction for robust embodied deployment.
6
Limitations and Future Work
The current scope of RMMBench is limited by the number of canonical task scenarios and the granularity of the atomic skill library. The navigation pipeline adopts a discretized grid abstraction, which isolates spatial reasoning but oversimplifies unstructured real-world terrain. The manipulation pipeline employs a frozen, off-the-shelf GraspNet for generalization, introducing localized grasping errors. Moreover, our controlled experiments show that grounding degradation with growing context length is model-dependent: only SEED-2.0 degrades persistently, exacerbated under long-distance and occlusion conditions. Future work will scale the task hierarchy, integrate head observation primitives, fine-tune perception-grasping modules, and further characterize the interplay between task horizon and grounding precision.

A.1
Atomic Skills
The library provides discrete primitives executed via an inverse kinematics (IK) solver. All manipulation skills are executed in a point-to-point manner, where low-level joint trajectory planning is resolved using a rapidly-exploring random tree (RRT) path planning solver. Parameterized skills primarily consist of pick
⟨
\langle
object
⟩
\rangle
and place to
⟨
\langle
container
⟩
\rangle

Non-parameterized manipulation skills include lift, pull, and push, which command the gripper to translate along fixed vertical or horizontal step sizes pre-configured by the system, while using the IK and RRT solvers to resolve the required 6-DoF end-effector trajectories. Sensory and state primitives include close_gripper, open_gripper, open_door, close_door, and observe, where observe resets the arm to a default canonical configuration to stabilize the visual field. Discrete navigation primitives regulate the mobile chassis coordinates in the environment layout and comprise move_forward, rotate_left, and rotate_right. The orientation skills command the chassis to execute fixed-angle rotations about its vertical axis.

To isolate reachable poses for the autonomously generated category, the raw GraspNet proposals undergo a four-layer filter. First, 3D grasps are projected onto the 2D image plane, removing candidates outside the predicted object bounding box. Second, a model-free collision detector discards any pose with a clearance distance under 0.01 m or an approach distance within 0.05 m against the scene point cloud. Third, candidates are screened based on depth and position percentiles within the camera frame, keeping the closest 10% of grasps while restricting the coordinates to the upper-middle regions. Fourth, the remaining valid candidates are ranked by their network score, preserving the top 4 highest-scoring configurations as actionable choices for the RRT-based execution.

Appendix D
Prompt Design
To effectively leverage the zero-shot reasoning capabilities of VLMs without fine-tuning, RMMBench implements a structured, state-conditioned prompting pipeline. Upon task initialization, the framework dispatches one of two distinct system prompt templates based on the underlying task paradigm. For localized interaction, models receive a dedicated manipulation template focusing on spatial grounding and end-effector coordinate selection. For long-horizon tasks, models receive a mobile navigation template (originally designed as composite_navigation) that explicitly decouples execution into sequential phases, establishing a logical priority where navigation and alignment must be completed before manipulation skills are triggered. To analyze multi-modal flexibility, the mobile navigation template dynamically adapts its instructional field based on sensor configurations, supporting both standard dual-camera inputs (mobile_navigation_no_bev) and three-view inputs augmented by a top-down bird’s-eye view (BEV) modality (mobile_navigation). Appendix E
Qualitative Analysis and Failure Modes
Figure E.1:
An example of execution blindness and viewpoint adjustment failure by SEED-2.0 during the “move the lemon from the pot to the bowl” task.
Figure E.2:
An example of a temporal-spatial reasoning deficit. The model fails to utilize historical visual context to maintain obstacle awareness once local 3D boundary features degrade.
Action Rigidity and Execution Blindness.
Although the models exhibit reasonable proficiency in high-level common-sense planning, they show a tendency to repeat identical action primitives rather than dynamically adjusting when localized execution failures occur. For instance, as shown in
Figure
E.1
, during the task “move the lemon from the pot to the bowl” executed by SEED-2.0, the model issued a place command, but the lemon failed to enter the bowl. Instead of calling an exploratory primitive like observe to regain a wide view and replan, the model directly repeated the pick command. Since the object was still within the field of view and recognized by the VLM, the model misjudged the current perspective as sufficient, which prompted it to directly repeat the pick command instead of invoking an observe action to reset its view. However, as a result of consecutive pick attempts, the wrist camera moved too close to the target. This narrow field of view led to highly inaccurate predictions of the bounding box. Since the system completely relies on bounding boxes for closed-loop grasping, blindly repeating pick under limited visibility and coordinate drift caused the robot to fall into a failure loop, revealing the model’s insufficiency in actively adjusting its viewpoint when local visibility degrades.
Temporal-Spatial Reasoning Deficit.
In composite navigation-manipulation tasks, the models’ capability to utilize historical image sequences to maintain current spatial awareness is fragile. For example, as shown in
Figure
E.2
, during the task “navigate to the open refrigerator to get the carrots” executed by Gemini-3-Flash, the white table obstacle ahead is clearly visible in the distant view during the early phase (e.g., in step a). However, as the mobile base executes the rotate_right and move_forward commands to approach the workstation (moving from step b to c), the white table loses its 3D boundary features in the head camera’s view, showing only a plain white surface. At this point, the model fails to utilize the distant visual memory from the sequence history to infer that the table still acts as an impassable obstacle ahead. Instead of adopting a strategy to bypass the obstacle, it continues to move forward and prematurely attempts to issue the local manipulation command pick with a target bounding box (step c and d). This failure in temporal-spatial reasoning eventually causes the robot to continuously collide forward into the obstacle and fail the grasp due to its inability to reach the target.
Figure E.3:
Examples of spatial grounding degradation over long execution steps, with both rollouts executed by SEED-2.0; intermediate place and observe skills are omitted. The model provides precise BBoxes in the early steps but suffers from severe coordinate hallucination and upward drift beyond Step 8.
E.1
Spatial Grounding Degradation in Long Sequences
To analyze the stability of 2D spatial grounding as interaction depth increases, we first examine a representative case and then verify its underlying cause through a controlled experiment. Note that the sequences in
Figure
E.3
only capture the wrist camera’s perspective and the corresponding object BBoxes during the execution of the pick skill, omitting the intermediate place and observe skills for brevity. As illustrated in
Figure
E.3
, which showcases two long-horizon tasks executed by SEED-2.0, the spatial grounding performance maintains high precision during the early steps of execution. In task (a), “Sort meat and vegetables”, the BBoxes tightly bound the carrot and tomato from Step 1 to Step 7. Similarly, in task (b), “Sort drinks by their packaging”, the model accurately locates the cola can, bottled cola, and the light-blue bottled drink from Step 1 to Step 6. However, as the task progresses beyond Step 8 and historical frames accumulate in the context window, a severe deterioration in spatial grounding becomes evident. By Step 10 in task (a) and Step 9 in task (b), the predicted BBoxes for the steak and the light-blue bottled drink exhibit extreme coordinate hallucinations, drifting completely away from the target objects and floating into the upper empty space of the frame, directly triggering critical grasping failures in the later phases of long-horizon tasks. This observation is consistent with the hypothesis that accumulated context attenuates the pixel-wise spatial fidelity of VLMs while largely preserving high-level semantic action selection.
To disentangle the relationship between context length and localization accuracy, this subsection provides the full details of the controlled experiment summarized in the main paper. We fix the visual input, vary only the number of history frames in the context (2–16), and evaluate the BBox IoU of each model under two conditions: a normal scene and a long-distance, occluded scene.

The results listed in Table
E.1
reveal that localization degradation is not a simple linear effect of context length but instead varies in a clearly model-dependent manner: models such as Gemini-3-Flash remain largely stable under the normal condition, whereas others fluctuate, though only within a narrow, trendless range; only SEED-2.0, the model shown in
Figure
E.3
, exhibits a clear persistent degradation as the context grows, which is further exacerbated and emerges earlier under the long-distance, occluded condition.
This indicates that context length itself is not the direct cause of localization degradation. The degradation is more likely jointly driven by the scene complexity (e.g., long distance and occlusion) and the disparity in visual robustness across models. For models sensitive to complex visual conditions, BBox accuracy degrades as the input image sequence grows longer, and grasp failures become correspondingly more frequent, whereas models with stronger visual robustness maintain stable low-level spatial resolution over long contexts.
