  <section id="heat">
    <h2>Why slow models overheat</h2>
    <p>While a model thinks, the robot holds its arms in place, and holding still heats the motors. When a motor reaches 65 °C, our thermal guard trips and returns the arms to rest pose for cooling.</p>

    <section id="choice">
      <h2>How we chose the tasks</h2>
      <ul class="limits">
        <li>We drafted 19 candidate tasks, each matched to a step quoted from a manufacturer manual or standard (Vertiv,
          Eaton, Dell, OSHA, WAGO and StarTech). They fell into four groups: operating controls, connecting equipment,
          servicing equipment, and the skills electricians are examined on.</li>
        <li>We kept ten single physical steps that a tabletop rig can reset and score from photos. That is six control tasks, one of them turning a key in an interlock, and four connection tasks.</li>
      </ul>

    <section id="limits">
      <h2>Limitations</h2>
      <ul class="limits">
        <li>The benchmark tests individual physical steps, not complete maintenance jobs. It does not cover electrical
          isolation, termination, testing or fault-finding.</li>
        <li>The props simplify the equipment, forces and access constraints. We have not validated performance on these setups against the real equipment.</li>
        <li>Each task ran on one rig. Differences between rigs (camera angles, how firmly a prop was fixed down) are part
          of each task's difficulty and are not averaged out.</li>
        <li>Three tries per model per task is a small sample. Scores a few points apart aren't a reliable ranking.</li>
        <li>Some failures, like misreading a knob's angle, may come from the 224 × 224 camera resolution.</li>
      </ul>
    </section>

      <h2>About Robocurve</h2>
      <p>Robocurve is a Public Benefit Corporation helping society understand the state of robot intelligence. We build
        open-source tools and independent benchmarks to evaluate how capable AI models and embodiments are in the real,
        physical world. These runs used <a href="https://github.com/robocurve/inspect-robots">Inspect Robots</a>, one of those tools.</p>
